The Dolomites, in Slow Motion

By Maren Holt March 14, 2026 12 min read 46.4°N · 11.8°E

Most people "do" the Dolomites in a day — a cable car, a photo, back on the bus. Give Alta Badia three slow days instead and it turns into the best mountain trip in Europe you don't need to be a climber to take.

This is the route I walk with friends who've never been. It sticks to one valley, leans on ski lifts to skip the steep bits, and books huts instead of hotels so dinner arrives with a view. You can fly into Venice or Innsbruck, take the train to Bolzano, and be eating polenta on a terrace by mid-afternoon.

Everything below is public-transport friendly — no rental car, no white-knuckle hairpins. Bring layers, a refillable bottle, and cash for the smaller rifugi.

The route, day by day

Three nights, two proper walks, and one deliberately lazy morning by a lake. Times are the ones that worked for us in late June; check the current lift and bus schedules before you go.

Day 01 · Arrival

Into the valley — Corvara & Colfosco

09:40
Train to Bolzano

The regional train from Verona climbs into the mountains — sit on the left. Swap to the SAD bus toward Corvara at the station.

13:00
Drop bags, quick lunch in Corvara

Check into the hut or guesthouse, then a plate of canederli (dumplings) in the village before the afternoon walk.

16:30
Colfosco meadow loop

A gentle 6 km warm-up under the Sella massif. Flat, flowery, and impossible to get lost on.

19:30
Dinner at the rifugio

Barley soup, speck and a local Lagrein. Early night — tomorrow starts before the crowds.

Day 02 · The big one

Sunrise on the ridge — Piz Boè shoulder

06:45
First cable car up

Take the earliest lift from Corvara toward the Boè plateau. You'll have the ridge almost to yourself for an hour.

08:30
Ridge traverse

A well-marked high path with cabled sections (via ferrata gloves help but aren't essential on this stretch). Pale spires in every direction.

13:00
Lunch at Rifugio Boè

2,871 m and open to walkers. The apple strudel is the reward you've been climbing toward.

17:00
Down to San Cassiano

Descend by lift and a short trail, then a slow evening in a quieter village than Corvara.

Day 03 · Slow morning

Lakes & a long way home

08:30
Bus to Lago di Braies

Go early — by 10:00 the car park at the famous emerald lake is full. A flat 3.5 km loop circles the water.

12:00
Last mountain lunch

One more terrace, one more strudel, then repack for the journey down.

15:00
Train home from Bolzano

You'll be back at sea level by dinner, already plotting the next valley.

Good to know before you go

  • Book huts (rifugi) months ahead for July–August; June and September are quieter and just as warm.
  • The Alta Badia lift pass pays for itself in a day if you're using cable cars to skip climbs.
  • Carry cash — smaller rifugi and mountain buses don't always take cards.
  • Afternoon thunderstorms are common; start walks early and be off exposed ridges by 14:00.
  • German, Italian and Ladin are all spoken here — "Bun dé" is hello in Ladin.
  • Refill bottles at village fountains; the water is cold, free and excellent.
